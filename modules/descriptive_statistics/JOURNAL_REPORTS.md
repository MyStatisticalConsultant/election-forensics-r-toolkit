The runner writes **one Word file per election**. Its main-text candidate is the initial compact national Tables 1–4; the complete district/municipality material is designed for **supplementary appendices**, not automatic insertion into a short journal paper. Never infer fraud from a table, star or p-value alone.

| Table/appendix | Included content | Analytical role |
|---|---|---|
| Table 1 | Registered, cast, valid, invalid and each group's share of the reference `All` totals | Scope and compositional accounting |
| Table 2 | Registered, number of stations, mean electorate per station | Whether apparent group differences follow station size |
| Table 3 | Turnout, first/second national option shares, effective number of options and robust turnout measures | Comparability, concentration, dispersion |

| Appendix C | **Executed** per-area tests with N, p, BH q and significance status | Inferential contextual follow-up |

Nationally first/second option identities remain the same for all areas. The `100 or more` row is explicitly shown **after `All`**, including appendices. This row equals `Main`; the alternative Size/JHK groups separately partition it. For presentation, absolute counts are integers, descriptive quantities and percentages have **two decimals**, and inferential p/q are shown with three decimals or `<0.001`. The underlying CSVs retain numerical precision.

## Choice of tests and multiple comparisons

Kruskal–Wallis compares station-level turnout and leading-option shares across **disjoint Small/Middle/Large** strata. Wilcoxon rank-sum compares **disjoint JHK Small/JHK Large**; large is the full complementary group. Tests require adequate station observations and variation. Missing p means non-estimable. BH corrections are performed within `Level × Outcome × Test`, across areas. Matrix Pearson BH corrections are **separate within each matrix**. Never mix these correction families or treat a small p-value as a sufficient anomaly verdict.
