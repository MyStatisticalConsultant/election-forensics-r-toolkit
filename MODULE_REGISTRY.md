Downstream modules independently read the prepared RZS workbooks; no downstream script changes them. KDE plots and contours are descriptive, not formal fraud tests. The voter-rigging module provides descriptive `D(p)` and cumulative vote-share analyses, not an inferential classification based on international `δ(p)` reference values. Approximate normality uses empirical fixed-bin distributions of rescaled logarithmic vote rates, with a separately labelled under-100 sensitivity analysis; it does not fit the parametric fraud model from Klimek et al. (2012). The Shpilkin excess-votes component is a model-dependent turnout scenario, not an independently identified fraud total; a zero estimate is not a clean-election verdict. The regression module presents station-level OLS associations for turnout and candidate support (A), and for invalid-ballot counts/rates and winner support (B1/B2), plus separate controlled sensitivity models. Its coefficients do not identify fraud or causal effects; the negative invalid-ballot association is a hypothesis, not a universal criterion. Other methods not listed here have not been implemented in this release.
